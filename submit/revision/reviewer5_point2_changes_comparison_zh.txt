This adjustment is motivated by the differences between privacy-preserving training and non-private training. For the learning rate, gradient clipping limits gradient magnitude, while noise injection perturbs the update direction and introduces additional variability. Ponomareva et al. [59] recommend retuning the learning rate when using a private optimizer and discuss how its choice depends on the clipping threshold. A moderately larger initial learning rate is adopted to provide sufficient step sizes for learning within a finite number of training epochs. For the learning-rate schedule, the non-private configuration in Section 4.2 reduces the learning rate after warm-up. Privacy-preserving training also needs to account for ongoing gradient perturbations and cumulative privacy expenditure. In their differentially private training experiments, Abadi et al. [49] started with a relatively high learning rate, reduced it linearly, and then held it constant. The ODIR experiments use this schedule to balance learning progress and update variability during the initial decay phase and to sustain learning of disease-discriminative features during the constant phase. The initial learning rate, the number of epochs of linear decay, and the final rate as a fraction of the initial rate were chosen based on our training experience with ODIR.
