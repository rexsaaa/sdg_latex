Gaussian differential privacy (GDP) provides (ε, δ)-differential privacy by adding independent and identically distributed Gaussian noise at each gradient update. For adjacent datasets D and D′, which differ by only one sample, a randomized algorithm M satisfies (ε, δ)-DP if, for any output set S, it satisfies:

Pr[M(D) ∈ S] ≤ e^ε Pr[M(D′) ∈ S] + δ,

where ε measures the degree of privacy preservation, and δ is the relaxation parameter, usually set to 10⁻⁵.

Differential privacy characterizes the influence of an individual training example on the output distribution of a randomized algorithm. The privacy definition in this study is formulated at the example level, where each example consists of a fundus image and its associated multi-label annotation. Two datasets D and D′ are adjacent when they differ in one training example. A randomized algorithm M satisfies (ε,δ)-DP if, for every pair of adjacent datasets and every measurable output set S,

Pr[M(D) ∈ S] ≤ e^ε Pr[M(D′) ∈ S] + δ,

Here, ε controls the multiplicative bound on output probabilities, while δ is the additive relaxation term. Under the same adjacency definition and δ, a smaller ε specifies a tighter privacy constraint. The privacy experiments in this study use δ=10⁻⁵ and report cumulative ε values alongside recognition performance.
