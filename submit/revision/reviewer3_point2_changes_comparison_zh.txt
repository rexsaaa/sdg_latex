2.2. Domain Generalization

In computer vision tasks, a model may perform well on training data but degrade when deployed on data collected by new institutions or devices because of differences in imaging conditions, illumination environments, and data distributions. This domain shift is especially prominent in medical imaging, where differences among hospitals, devices, and acquisition procedures can directly affect model generalization [23]. Domain Adaptation (DA) is a common approach for alleviating domain shift, with the core idea of aligning distributions using both source-domain and target-domain data during training. For example, maximum-mean-discrepancy-based Deep Domain Confusion [24] and the correlation alignment method CORAL [25] improve transfer performance by reducing feature distribution differences. DANN [26] uses adversarial training to learn domain-invariant features. CycleGAN [27] and related unsupervised domain adaptation methods [28] alleviate domain shift through image style transfer or cross-domain contrastive learning. However, DA usually requires access to target-domain data during training, an assumption that often fails when medical data are privacy-sensitive or data from the target institution cannot be obtained in advance.

（下一段开头两句，上下文保持不变）
Unlike DA, Domain Generalization (DG) aims to learn predictive models using only source-domain data for application to unseen target domains [29]. Domain-invariant representation learning is one approach to this task, aiming to reduce the influence of domain-related variations on the learned features.
