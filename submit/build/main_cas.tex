\begin{abstract}
Early fundus screening is effective for ocular disease diagnosis. Deep learning has provided tools for fundus image analysis.
However, clinical deployment still faces three major challenges: (1) multiple diseases often co-occur in a single eye, causing false positives and false negatives when modeled independently; (2) cross-institutional deployment suffers from domain shift, yet conventional cross-domain methods typically require target-domain or multi-source data, often infeasible; (3) medical data are privacy-sensitive, further restricting cross-institutional sharing and invalidating the multi-source assumption, calling for generalization with local privacy preservation.
To address these three challenges in order, we first propose an Adaptive Label-Feature Relevance Construction (ARC) module that captures disease co-occurrence dependencies and links each disease label to the corresponding image features via Transformer attention to reduce false positives and negatives.
Then, we design a Dual-Branch Guided Domain-Invariant Feature Extraction (DFE) module that learns transferable features from a single source domain under Class Activation Mapping (CAM) guidance, improving unseen-domain generalization and interpretability.
Finally, we integrate an Adaptive Gaussian Differential Privacy (AdaGDP) mechanism into training, preserving privacy through dynamic gradient noise injection with controlled performance loss.
Experiments show that our method achieves a Final score of 78.85 on the ODIR Off-site set, outperforming representative multi-label ocular disease recognition methods.
\revision{In comparisons with established domain generalization methods, our method also achieves the highest Final scores in both the in-domain evaluation on ODIR On-site set and the cross-domain evaluation on EDID dataset.}
\revision{With AdaGDP, our method protects source-domain training information while achieving recognition performance comparable to that of the official non-private ODIR baseline.}
In summary, we establish an interpretable and privacy-preserving single-domain generalization framework for multi-label ocular disease recognition, improving cross-domain performance and providing insights into ocular diagnosis under privacy constraints.
Code is available at \href{https://github.com/rexsaaa/ppisdg-ocular}{https://github.com/rexsaaa/ppisdg-ocular}.
\end{abstract}
